- Russian Empire -- one of 10 gubernias:
   - Bessarabia
   - Podolia
   - Volhynia
   - Kherson
   - Kiev
   - Ekaterinoslav
   - Chernigov
   - Kharkov 
   - Poltava
   - Taurida
  
- Austrian Empire:
   - Galicia
      - Lwów
      - Tarnopol
      - Stanislawów
   - Bukovina
   - Transcarpathia
  
These are the borders as of 1900 -- with the exception
of Austrian Galicia, for which we are using the inter-war 
provincial subdivisions (województwa) of Poland.

JewishGen Communities Database:
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Here are the specs to use for towns currently in Ukraine:


1900:
~~~~

- The "Country" is:
      either "Russian Empire" or "Austrian Empire" or "Hungary".
- The "Province" is:
      for "Russian Empire" -- one of the 10 gubernias:
         "Bessarabia", "Chernigov", "Ekaterinoslav", "Kharkov", 
         "Kherson", "Kiev", "Podolia", "Poltava", "Taurida", 
         or "Volhynia" (and possible others, outside the Pale).
      for "Austrian Empire" -- one of either:
         "Bukovina", or "Galicia".
      for "Hungary" -- one of:
         * "Kárpátalja" (for Bereg and Ung megyes),
         * "Máramaros"  (for Máramaros and Ugocsa megyes).
- The "District" is:
      for "Russian Empire" -- an uyezd (see below)
      for "Austrian Empire" -- ???
      for "Hungary" -- one of 4 megyes:
         "Maramaros", "Bereg", "Ugocsa" or "Ung".
- The "Town" name would be:
      for "Russian Empire" -- 
         * in the Russian language (transliterated from Cyrillic).
      for "Austria-Hungary" -- 
         * in the German language.
      for "Hungary" --
         * in the Hungarian language.

Uyezds of the Russian Empire:
   There are about 10 uyezds (districts) per gubernia (province).
   Sources are:
    - "Evrejskaia Entsiklopediia" ["Jewish Encyclopaedia"],
        (St. Petersburg: Brokhaus-Efron, 1906-1913),
        in the article on each gubernia.
        There is also an article on each uyezd, which contains
        the 1897 population figures for all major Jewish towns
        within that uyezd.
        http://brockhaus-efron-jewish-encyclopedia.ru/
    - "Vsia Rossiia" - Russian Business Directories, 1895-1911.
        Partial indexes online on JewishGen:
        < http://www.jewishgen.org/databases/vsia >.
    - "A Dictionary of Jewish Surnames from the Russian Empire"
        by Alexander Beider.  Revised Edition (Avotaynu, 1993, 2008).
        See map on page xxi, and list of uyezds of following pages
        (xxii-xxvii).
        The same map appears in "Where Once We Walked" 
        (Avotaynu, 2002 Revised Edition), page xxviii.
    - The 1911 "Encyclopaedia Britannica", OCR-scanned text 
        at < http://encyclopedia.jrank.org/ >. 
        See the articles on each gubernia.
    - "Encyclopedia of Ukraine" website.  Incomplete, but see:
        http://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?AddButton=pages\G\U\Gubernia.htm,  
        http://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?AddButton=pages\C\H\Chernihivgubernia.htm
        http://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?AddButton=pages\K\A\Katerynoslavgubernia.htm
        http://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?AddButton=pages\K\H\Khersongubernia.htm
    - Maps of the Russian Empire (in Cyrillic):
        http://www.genealogia.ru/projects/maps/maps/alf.html

   Kiev gubernia:
         See < http://www.jewishgen.org/databases/Ukraine/KievDuma.htm >:
         12 uyezds: Berdichev, Cherkassy, Chigirin, Kanev, Kiev, Lipovets,
         Radomysl, Skvira, Tarashcha, Uman, Vasilkov, Zvenigorodka.
   Bessarabia gubernia:
         See < http://www.jewishgen.org/databases/Romania/BessarabiaDuma.htm >:
         3 uyezds all or partially within modern Ukraine:
         Akkerman, Izmail, Khotin
         (the remainer of Bessarabia's uyezds are in modern Moldova).
   Podolia gubernia:
         See < http://www.jewishgen.org/Ukraine/Podolia/Podolia.xls >,
         < http://www.shtetlinks.jewishgen.org/Litin/taxes.html >, 
         < http://www.shtetlinks.jewishgen.org/Litin/taxes1883-3.html >.

   Minsk gubernia:
         Also note that some small portions of SW Minsk Gubernia 
         (which is predominantly in Belarus) are now in Ukraine. 
         i.e.: parts of Mozyr and Pinsk uyezds.  
         See map #40a in Magocsi, page 136.
         Can check towns included using JCDB.

        "Poland Volume V: Volhynia and Polesie", 1990;

        http://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/pinkas_poland/pinkas_poland2.html ;

        previosuly southern Podolia and western Kherson gubernias], see:
        "Romania Volune I", 1969, pp. 349-545,
        http://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/pinkas_romania/rom1_00000.html ;
      - For Romanian Ukraine [small portions of Bessarabia], see:
        "Romania Volume II", 1980, pp. 279-418.
      
      "Encyclopedia of Jewish Life Before and During the Holocaust", 
      edited by Shmuel Spector.  An English-language consolidation of 
      "Pinkas HaKehillot", published by Yad Vashem (3 volumes, 
      1,824 pages, c 2001).
      This will probably be the best source of inter-war
      district names for towns now in Ukraine, which are
      not covered by the above sources.

Chernigov Gubernia:
    15 uyezds -- most are in northern Ukraine, 
        but 4 are now all or partly in modern Russia:
        Mglin, Novozybkov, Starodub, Surazh.
        These are Chernigov Gubernia's four northernmost uyezds.
    There are 14 Jewish communities now in modern Russia:    
    
    Churovichi (Novozybkov uyezd)
    Klimovo (Novozybkov uyezd)
    Klintsy (Surazh uyezd)
    Krasnaya Gora (Surazh uyezd)
    Mglin (Mglin uyezd)
    Novozybkov (Novozybkov uyezd)
    Pochëp (Mglin uyezd)
    Pogar (Starodub uyezd) 
    Starodub (Starodub uyezd) 
    Surazh (Surazh uyezd)
    Svyatsk (Surazh uyezd)
    Unecha (Surazh uyezd)
    Voronok (Starodub uyezd) 
    Zlynka (Novozybkov uyezd)
